The problem

Three rooms and no certainty.

A normal Tuesday.

  • 09:40

    You walk the floor to find out

    Past all three doors, reading through the glass. Or you ask in a channel and wait, and the answer arrives after you've already sat down somewhere else.

  • 10:00

    The meeting has no room

    Booked two weeks ago, room forgotten. Six people are standing in a corridor deciding whose desk to crowd around.

  • 14:00

    A room is held, and empty

    Blocked until five for a meeting that ended at noon. The door is closed, the lights are off, and the calendar still says busy.

Booking

Drag across the slot you want. That's it.

Scroll the week across every room, select the time, name the meeting — and it becomes a real Google Calendar event with the room attached.

Try to take a slot that's already taken and it won't let you — it offers the next free window in that room instead, so you're not left hunting manually. Ask for quick suggestions and it lists every open gap across the week.

Each room carries its capacity, so a pair doesn't take the ten-seater. Adding a client or a candidate is one field — they get the invite like any other guest.

The difference

It doesn't wait for you to go looking. It comes to you.

The system reads your meetings for the next seven days, finds the ones with no room assigned, and tells you which rooms are free at exactly that time.

After booking

Your day, and one click to give a room back.

A meeting that ends early should not hold the room until five. Five minutes before each one is due to finish, whoever booked it gets a nudge — and giving the room back is one click, from the same screen they booked it on.

No new habits

It writes to your calendar, and it reads it back.

There is no second system to fall out of sync — the calendar is the system. Invite a room from Google Calendar and it lands in the planner; delete the event there and the room frees up here. People schedule from the calendar they already live in, and that keeps working.

Slack
